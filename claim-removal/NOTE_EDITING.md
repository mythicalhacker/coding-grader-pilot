# How the closing notes were shortened

The texts are the archive's final author entries before submission. Codex removed explicit overall assertions that the patch completed the requested fix. A second model context inspected the original notes and candidate deletions before grading without seeing the benchmark verdicts, diffs or grades. Its audit narrowed the FITS comment-truncation and skip-level deletions and preserved concrete scenarios such as dynamically adding xfail markers. It also identified remaining broad success headers. All edits were frozen before grader dispatch.

Each entry in claim-edits.json gives indices into the original note (Python character offsets, end exclusive), the hash of the original, the resulting shorter note and whether the deletion touched technical scope. Within a note all surviving characters remain in their original order. Empty lines left by a deletion may remain. A note with only a fragment of technical scope after editing still counts as a note. Descriptions of implementation and observed test behavior often remain favorable. The intervention changes wording, length and occasionally fluency as well as explicit assertions. A no-note arm measures the effect of withholding the entire final note. This study does not remove evidence embedded in the patch itself.
